Short answer: In Croatia, e-invoices between companies have been mandatory since January 1, 2026, in Slovenia they become mandatory on January 1, 2028, and in Serbia e-invoices are already in use and e-delivery notes (eOtpremnica) are coming. In the Federation of Bosnia and Herzegovina, the new fiscalization law starts to apply by the end of August 2027 at the latest. An invoice is no longer a PDF sent by email but structured data that travels through the state system or an authorized intermediary, so a company that retypes data from Excel into an invoice by hand has to change that before the deadline.

If you sell in several countries of the region, there is not one rule but several, with different dates. That is why it helps to know what exactly is changing, where the deadlines are and what a company must put in order so that an invoice is not rejected. The situation in this article is as of November 26, 2026, and it is not legal advice.

What is an e-invoice and how is it different from a PDF?

An e-invoice is an invoice in a structured form, most often XML, that a computer can read and process by itself. A PDF sent by email looks like an invoice, but to the system it is just an image of a document. An e-invoice is not sent by ordinary mail but through the state system or via an authorized intermediary, and the data in it can also reach the tax authority.

This changes how you look at data too. The buyer's name, tax number, address, items and VAT must be accurate and recorded in the same way every time, because the system checks them automatically.

Which deadlines apply by country?

Here is an overview for the markets where we work and sell. Deadlines change, so before you decide, check the current situation with your accountant and in the official sources of the tax authority.

CountryWhat appliesDeadline and note
CroatiaFiscalization 2.0 and e-invoices in business-to-business transactionsFrom January 1, 2026, VAT-registered companies issue and receive e-invoices, while others receive them from 2026 and issue them from January 1, 2027. The invoice goes through an information intermediary (an e-invoicing service provider), and the data is reported to the tax administration.
SerbiaE-invoices through the SEF system and eOtpremnicaE-invoices are already mandatory between companies. eOtpremnica (e-delivery note): from January 1, 2026 for the public sector and excise goods, and for other business entities from October 1, 2027.
SloveniaE-invoices between companies (the ZIERDED law)From January 1, 2028. It must be structured XML (for example e-SLOG), a PDF does not count. Exchange goes through authorized providers or the PEPPOL network.
Federation of Bosnia and HerzegovinaLaw on fiscalization of transactions, a central platformThe law has been in force since February 12, 2026, and application begins once the implementing regulations are adopted, by August 31, 2027 at the latest. E-invoices between companies come later, with a transition period.
Republika SrpskaElectronic fiscalization already existsWe have not found a new deadline for e-invoices between companies. Follow the tax administration.
Austria and the EUE-invoices in public procurement, an EU plan for cross-border transactionsThere is not yet a general obligation between companies in Austria. The EU plans e-invoicing in cross-border transactions from July 2030.

In Croatia and Slovenia the obligation applies to domestic transactions, while invoicing abroad is for now a matter of agreement. Still, if you have customers or suppliers in those countries, it is better to open the e-invoice conversation before the deadline than after it.

Why is this a problem for a company that works in Excel?

While an invoice was paper or a PDF, a retyping error was usually fixed by phone. When a system processes the invoice, an error can mean a rejected invoice and late payment. The most common causes are very ordinary:

  • Customer data is retyped by hand. A wrong tax number or address and the invoice does not go through.
  • The same item has several names. The system requires unambiguous codes and VAT rates.
  • The order, delivery note and invoice are three separate spreadsheets. Nobody knows for certain what has been delivered but not invoiced.
  • The knowledge sits with one person. When they are not there, the invoice is late.

Fines are prescribed for such failures, and the amounts differ by country, so check them with your accountant.

An invoice has become data that travels through the state system. That is why a retyping error is no longer a small thing.

What should a small business do now?

  1. Check which deadline applies to you. Look at the country where you are registered, but also at the countries of your biggest customers and suppliers.
  2. Ask your accountant and your accounting software vendor how e-invoices will be sent and received: through which intermediary or platform, and at what cost.
  3. Clean up your data. Make one list of customers and items with accurate tax numbers, addresses, codes and VAT rates.
  4. Connect the flow. The order, work order, delivery note and invoice should be one whole, not four files.
  5. Try it on a few invoices months before the deadline, so errors are found while they do not yet cost anything.

What can custom software solve, and what can it not?

Custom software does not replace your accounting program, does not issue fiscal invoices and does not replace certified fiscal equipment. That stays in your accountant's program or with an authorized intermediary.

What it can do is put in order the place where the data is created. The customer and items are entered once, the order carries the data through production to the delivery note, and the system immediately shows what has been delivered but not invoiced. That way already checked data arrives in the invoice, without retyping. That is the part of the work that pays off most to sort out before the deadline.

Frequently asked questions

Is a PDF invoice sent by email still valid?

It depends on the country. In Croatia, VAT-registered companies issue and receive e-invoices between themselves from January 1, 2026. In Slovenia, from January 1, 2028 a PDF will not count as an e-invoice. In Bosnia and Herzegovina there is for now no general obligation for e-invoices between companies.

Do I need to worry if I only sell in Bosnia and Herzegovina?

Less, but do not ignore it. In the Federation of Bosnia and Herzegovina the fiscalization law is already in force, and application follows by the end of August 2027 at the latest. If you export or buy from Croatia, Serbia or Slovenia, your partners will expect orderly and accurate data.

Does Datamaks issue e-invoices or fiscalize?

No. We build the flow from the order through the work order and delivery note to the data for the invoice. The issuing itself goes through your accounting program or an authorized intermediary.

What about invoices to other countries?

In Croatia and Slovenia the obligation applies to domestic transactions. For export, you and the customer agree on the form of the invoice, but structured data makes the work easier in any case.
